# Which architecture firms shaped multiple Australian university campuses, and how were they connected to each other?

**Collection:** BMAU -- Campus: Building Modern Australian Universities | **Discipline:** Architectural history

Rachel, an architectural historian studying the postwar expansion of Australian universities, opens the BMAU collection knowing that the decades after 1945 saw an extraordinary building programme -- dozens of entirely new campuses raised from paddocks and bushland within a single generation. The collection, created by the Melbourne School of Design, documents 42 universities, 366 campuses, and 458 architect relationships alongside 150 landscape architect entries and 112 planner records. She is interested in something the individual campus histories rarely reveal: the small group of firms whose commissions spanned state borders and institutional rivalries, quietly establishing a shared architectural language across universities that imagined themselves as distinct. *Can I see which architecture firms appear in connection with the most campuses, and whether any of them worked at universities in different states?* The answer surfaces quickly. Eggleston, Macdonald & Secomb appears at seven campuses: the Australian National University in Acton, both the Caulfield and Clayton campuses of Monash University, Swinburne in Hawthorn, the University of Melbourne at Parkville, La Trobe at Bundoora, and Federation University at Mount Helen in Ballarat. John Andrews International reaches across six campuses in four states -- from the ANU in Canberra to Griffith in Nathan, Queensland University of Technology at Kelvin Grove, the University of Southern Queensland in Toowoomba, RMIT in Melbourne, and the University of Canberra at Bruce. These are not local practices filling local briefs; they are firms whose reach shaped what an Australian university looks like.

Rachel turns her attention to the Melbourne campuses, where the overlap is densest. La Trobe at Bundoora alone lists connections to Roy Simpson, Chancellor & Patrick, Sir Roy Grounds, Robin Boyd, Stephenson & Turner, Eggleston Macdonald & Secomb, Bates Smart & McCutcheon, and Mockridge Stahle & Mitchell. Many of these same names reappear at Monash Clayton and Melbourne Parkville. *I wonder if I can trace a single firm -- say Mockridge, Stahle & Mitchell -- and see every campus they touched?* The collection shows them at five campuses: Federation University in Ballarat, both La Trobe campuses at Bundoora and Bendigo, the ANU in Canberra, and Monash Clayton. Sir Roy Grounds connects to three -- Melbourne Parkville, Monash Clayton, and La Trobe Bundoora -- while Robin Boyd appears at both La Trobe and Melbourne. The picture that emerges is of a tight web of Melbourne-based practices, many of them personal acquaintances or former colleagues, seeding their Brutalist and late-Modernist vocabularies across Victoria's new campuses and reaching into the national capital.

What makes the collection especially valuable for Rachel's work is that it records not only architect relationships but also planner and landscape connections, and it tracks the way firms evolve over time through explicit name-change records. Hassell, McConnell & Partners, who planned the University of Canberra at Bruce and designed buildings at Flinders University in Bedford Park and the University of South Australia at Mawson Lakes, is recorded as formerly known as Hassell -- the same practice that later designed the Tonsley campus for Flinders under its shortened name. Bates, Smart & McCutcheon, who worked at La Trobe, RMIT, and Victoria University, are linked to their successor firm Bates Smart. *Can I follow these name changes to see the full span of a single practice's university work across its various incarnations?* The Formerly_known_as and Later_known_as relationships in the collection make this possible, connecting what might otherwise look like separate firms into continuous threads of practice.

The connections between individuals and firms open yet another dimension. Roy Simpson, described in the collection as "best known for his long involvement with the practice Yuncken Freeman," trained at the University of Melbourne architectural atelier and became a named partner at Yuncken Freeman. He is recorded as an architect at La Trobe Bundoora, and separately Yuncken Freeman Architects appears as planner at La Trobe, at the Australian Defence Force Academy campus of the University of New South Wales in Canberra, and at Charles Darwin University in Casuarina. The collection explicitly links Simpson to Yuncken Freeman through a bidirectional architect relationship. Meanwhile, landscape architects like Lindsay Pryor appear at both the ANU's main campus in Acton and the ADFA campus in Canberra, suggesting that the physical setting of a university -- its trees, lawns, and sightlines -- could also carry a signature across institutions. *Could I bring together all the architects, planners, and landscape professionals who touched a single campus like the ANU at Acton and see who among them also shaped other universities?* That question would reveal not just who built Canberra's university, but how the ANU sat within a national web of professional relationships -- the same hands, the same eyes, moving from one master plan to the next, carrying ideas about what a modern Australian campus should be.
